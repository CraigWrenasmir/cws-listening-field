## Planned chapter — Distant Rooms, Op. 341–350

The following are compositional intentions for sequential development, not completed works or heard results. Exact keys, two-word titles and catalogue ancestry will be selected as each work is composed. Each early altered note should cause a later destination or change the meaning of a return; if removing it leaves the harmonic journey equally convincing, its role needs further development. Retain two hands, six pages and 768 sounded pitch onsets maximum, usually 2–5 minutes. The proportions below are approximate planning aids, not templates.

| Opus | Harmonic mechanism | Distinct form, texture and ending |
|---|---|---|
| 341 | A chromatic neighbour gains bass authority, becomes a dominant root and opens a distant tonic. | Through-composed migration, roughly 25:45:30. The melody fragments as the bass gains agency. End quietly at the new destination with a recognisable opening interval, without a homeward reprise. |
| 342 | An early raised sixth in minor becomes the third of a substantial major subdominant region, then remains as the sixth of the recovered minor tonic. | Unequal variations over a returning bass phrase, roughly 20:15:30:25:10. Exchange the melody between hands and change which bass notes remain audible. Close on a carefully voiced minor-sixth sonority. |
| 343 | An early raised fourth becomes the leading note of the dominant, allowing that dominant to establish itself as a rival home. | Compressed sonata process, roughly 30:45:25, with an abbreviated recap. Passagework carries the tonal decisions. At the return, resolve the altered note inside a recovered tonic sonority. |
| 344 | An early lowered seventh in major becomes the third of the minor dominant, changing the expressive behaviour of the principal phrase. | Asymmetrical two-panel design, roughly 40:60, plus a short afterthought. Move from chords to exposed independent lines. Return only the opening cadence, with the borrowed note resolving towards a warm sixth chord. |
| 345 | A borrowed lowered sixth above a recurring tonic bass eventually becomes a new tonic when the bass moves beneath it. | A passacaglia with three increasingly unequal cycles, then release from the ground. Use held inner notes, displaced bass entries and register exchanges. Finish with the bass absent but the destination audible in the remaining voices. |
| 346 | Preserve an early chromatic melodic pitch while its harmonic role changes from tension to stable chord member across different tonal regions. | Continuous cantilena over three differently paced fields, roughly 15:50:35. The middle voice carries the reference pitch while the outer voices travel. End with a simple resolved phrase; avoid making the reference pitch a continuous pedal. |
| 347 | Establish a diminished-seventh chord with its expected resolution, then reinterpret the same pitch collection enharmonically to enter a distant minor tonic. | Branching form: a short first outcome, interrupted expansion and a longer second outcome. Exposed counterpoint makes the voice leading audible. End at the distant destination without hurried tonic restoration. Reserve this mechanism for one work. |
| 348 | Two voices disagree about one altered pitch: one treats it as a neighbour requiring resolution, the other as a stable chord member. Their disagreement shapes the later tonal centre. | Interleaved episodes whose lengths gradually converge, followed by a brief shared statement. Unequal phrase endings and held notes beneath moving lines provide technical demands. Reconcile through a common chord and finish in tempo. |
| 349 | A chromatic implication survives several apparently complete cadences; its eventual resolution changes the meaning of an earlier phrase. | Three unequal incomplete endings followed by one short complete ending. Alternate spacious chords and exposed single lines. Let the decisive release occur quietly, through precise voicing rather than increased density. |
| 350 | A note initially heard as a chromatic ornament gradually reveals itself as the leading note of the eventual tonic; the complete theme explains the opening fragments retrospectively. | Reverse emergence: fragment, partial song, interruption, complete song, roughly 10:25:20:45. Move from separated registers to an integrated singing texture. Close with a plain cadence and breathing space, through recognition rather than grandeur. |

Advanced difficulty should arise from voicing, releases, sustained independence and control of long phrases as appropriate to each work. None of these intentions requires exhausting the page or onset allowance. Notation and technical validation will not establish the artistic success of the intended harmonic consequences; listening and keyboard assessment remain distinct.
