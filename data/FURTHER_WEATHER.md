The following are compositional intentions for sequential development, not completed works or heard results. Exact keys, two-word titles and catalogue ancestry will be selected as each work is composed. Each early altered note should cause a later destination or change the meaning of a return; if removing it leaves the harmonic journey equally convincing, its role needs further development. Retain two hands, six pages and 768 sounded pitch onsets maximum, usually 2–5 minutes. The proportions below are approximate planning aids, not templates.

## Planned chapter — Interwoven Songs, Op. 351–360

These are independent design proposals for sequential composition, not completed works, approved musical results or listening judgements. They were shaped against the authored sources for Op. 331–349: cross-rhythms, held inner voices, hand exchanges and unequal-phrase convergence already have substantial precedents. This chapter should make recognisable, independently singable subjects cause the development through their actual combination. Exact keys, ancestry, phrase lengths and final titles remain composition decisions. Retain warm melancholy, resolving jazz colour, two hands, six pages and 768 sounded pitch onsets maximum, usually 2–5 minutes.

The ten proposed two-word titles were checked against all 350 titles in `data/catalog.json`, ending with Velvet Distance, on 19 September 2026: no exact case-insensitive collisions. Recheck before integration.

| Opus / proposed title | Contrapuntal process | Suggested form, metre, texture and ending |
|---|---|---|
| 351 — Quince Tandem | Invertible duet. Introduce a long-breathed subject A and a rhythmically distinct subject B separately; combine them, then exchange their upper/lower positions at the octave. | 3/4, with unequal subject lengths. Expositions lead to two differently spaced combined spans; the changed bass role must alter the harmonic reading. End with a short shared cadence, without a complete solo reprise. Check the inverted vertical intervals before adding decoration. |
| 352 — Larch Crossroads | Augmentation. After separate statements, double A's written durations in the lower voice while B retains its own melodic pace above it. | Broad 6/4. Let the augmented statement form the longest uninterrupted region; B develops across it instead of looping unchanged. Finish with B withdrawing before A completes its last phrase. Difficulty lies in sustaining the slower voice through the other's movement. |
| 353 — Briar Braid | Metrical counterpoint between two complete songs: A has three quarter-note pulses; B has two dotted-quarter pulses over the same elapsed span. | Introduce A in 3/4 and B in 6/8, then combine them within a common notated bar span at unchanged quarter-note speed. Reverse which song carries the foreground once. End with a brief shared three-pulse cadence. Written grouping and phrasing, not an independent playback clock, create the distinction. |
| 354 — Celandine Interleaf | Contrary-motion transformation. Expose A and B, then combine an inverted A with original B; later let original A meet inverted B. | 5/4, in an expanding arch with no complete opening reprise. Delimit the inverted subject material precisely and distinguish exact inversion from any deliberate tonal adjustment. Keep two exposed lines, using wider spacing at the centre. Close with separate stepwise resolutions into a quiet third or sixth. |
| 355 — Bluebell Commonage | Three-subject assembly. Hear A, B and C separately, then explore A+B, B+C and A+C before one substantial three-voice meeting. | 9/8. Give the subjects different rhythms and registers; use pairwise passages to prepare the later harmony. Limit continuous three-voice writing to the consequential meeting rather than maintaining maximum texture. Let one subject finish alone after the others have completed their phrases. |
| 356 — Ash Colloquy | Stretto with a contrasting countersubject. Establish A and B separately; overlapping entries of A progressively shorten their entry distance, before A and B receive a clear combined statement. | Flowing 2/2. Short episodes allow the imitative pressure to rise in stages; preserve complete subject identity in at least one overlap. Use mostly two voices and resist an automatic volume increase. Release into a compact, plain A+B ending in tempo. |
| 357 — Dogwood Accord | Prepared suspensions. A mobile song and a slower, independently shaped song B are first exposed; combining them lets B's held notes cross A's harmonic changes and resolve in the same voice. | Spacious 3/2. Build an evolving chain of selected 7–6, 9–8 or 4–3 events rather than one climactic block. Prepare and resolve each intended suspension explicitly. Finish with different release times: the active voice settles, then the remaining note completes its own motion. |
| 358 — Teasel Exchange | Hocket becoming overlap. A and B each have characteristic rests; their first combination fills those spaces into a continuous composite line. Later, selected notes sustain across the other voice's entry. | 7/8, with phrase-specific grouping. Separate statements lead to interlocking motion, then a more legato overlapping region. Preserve each subject's recognisable rhythm through the change. End with two short, differently timed gestures and a full release. Hands must articulate the exchanges without relying on pedal to join every gap. |
| 359 — Lupin Rendezvous | Retrograde encounter. Establish A and B, expose a clearly delimited phrase of A with its pitch and duration order reversed, then combine that reversed phrase with B. | 4/4, with deliberately unequal sections. The changed direction needs new voice leading rather than the original accompaniment played underneath it. Describe only the verified phrase as retrograde, not the whole piece. Let B complete the final song after the reversed material falls away. |
| 360 — Bilberry Chorus | Double fugato. Give A and B their own short imitative expositions, then sustain their combined development, with a later exchange of register and emphasis. | Begin in 4/4 and broaden into 6/4 only for the final combined phrase. Each exposition must establish a distinct melodic identity; the combined region should be the largest single span. Thin the texture into a quiet duet whose voices finish independently. Avoid a compulsory grand ending or a catalogue collage. |

Before expanding each score, identify the exact subject statements and test the decisive combined passage as explicit voices. Preserve enough of both subjects' pitch and rhythmic identity for their relationship to be legible; a hand exchange or transposition alone does not establish the intended process. Resolve incidental collisions deliberately, distinguish sustained finger occupation from pedal resonance, and check all simultaneous hand spans and shared keys. Prefer exposed independence and selective three-voice writing to greater note density. The proposed metres serve phrase behaviour and can change if the authored music warrants it; they are not variety quotas. Engraving and technical checks remain separate from listening and keyboard assessment.
