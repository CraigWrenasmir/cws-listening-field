# Singing Returns — CWS Op. 321–330

Ten original studies of a melody returning with changed expression, informed by the discussion of Chopin Op. 9 No. 2. The reference supplies compositional principles, not quoted musical material. Stable grounds, singing phrases, directed chromaticism, selective ornamentation and a deliberate emotional peak are explored within the CWS sound.

| Opus | Title | Statement starts (bars) | Experiment |
|---|---|---|---|
| 321 | Willow Cantilena | 1, 7, 17 | Six-bar cantilena: plain, ornamented, then reharmonised. |
| 322 | Myrtle Reverie | 1, 13, 25 | Unequal breaths, cross-bar ties, a delayed last entry and an exposed closing note. |
| 323 | Amber Promenade | 1, 9, 21 | Offbeat chords, written dotted turns and a bright major-seventh ending. |
| 324 | Birch Aubade | 1, 13, 25 | A gentle waltz, borrowed minor colour and a descending final bass. |
| 325 | Sedge Lantern | 1, 9, 21 | A 9/8 song with suspensions and a chromatically climbing central bass. |
| 326 | Iris Portico | 1, 13, 25 | Left-hand cantilena beneath quiet chords; staff dynamics bring the melody forward. |
| 327 | Clover Reverie | 1, 13, 25 | Bell-like intervals and a restrained high point; four bars of unaccompanied return. |
| 328 | Reed Noctilucence | 1, 9, 17 | Five-beat phrases over 3+2, 2+3 and sustained chordal support. |
| 329 | Alder Remanence | 1, 9, 17 | A single melody becomes a duet in sixths and thirds, then sheds its doubling. |
| 330 | Orchid Reverie | 1, 9, 23 | A decorated return followed by rhythmic augmentation from 4/4 into 6/4. |

Each score was completed and visually inspected before composing the next. The ten works total 2,943 sounded pitch onsets, 39 printed pages and 1,274.88 seconds of recordings including room tails. Each remains within the Second Studies limits of six pages and 768 sounded pitches.

The recordings retain the existing light room reverb and shared phrase tempo map. Melodic freedom is authored in the notation: delayed entries, sustained notes, rests, ornaments and rhythmic augmentation. No new independent-hand timing system is claimed.

Every work has a documented pitch relationship with an earlier catalogue piece. Op. 329 is a child of Op. 321, allowing the set to develop its own small branch in Kinship. All works also enter the five complete listening routes and Volume 15 downloads.

Score/MIDI agreement, timing, bar lengths, engraving, pedal releases, complete held-hand spans, separate key occupation and decoded recording checks passed individually.
